The gate’s decisive‑ness collapses when you try to stretch it beyond the two canned benchmarks. Its entire “cheap route sufficient” branch rests on a **0.5% upper bound for between‑seed sigma** estimated from only `libquantum` and `bzip2` (both single‑threaded, tiny, call‑heavy C programs). Whether layout variance can be crushed below that bound on *those two* tells you nothing about whether the cheap lld‑padding + DieHard cocktail works on real, diverse workloads. The decision to abandon the full port (or not) cannot be reliably taken from such an under‑powered experiment – that makes the gate unsound in the “generalisation” sense and thus not a well‑posed rule for the overall resurrection decision.

If the cheap route passes on two cherry‑picked workloads, the gate declares it “sufficient” without any check that the result holds elsewhere.
